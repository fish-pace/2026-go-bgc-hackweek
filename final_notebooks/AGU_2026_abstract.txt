Title: Revealing Subsurface Phytoplankton Structure in the Ocean using Hyperspectral Ocean Color Data, in-situ Measurements and Machine-Learning

Current remote-sensing products provide global estimates of near-surface chlorophyll-a, but phytoplankton and the marine food webs they support are distributed throughout the water column. Understanding this vertical structure is important for identifying deep chlorophyll maxima and improving estimates of total water-column chlorophyll, which are valuable for fish habitat modeling and carbon cycle models. To extend satellite observations below the surface, we developed CHLA-Z, an experimental framework for estimating chlorophyll-a profiles to 200 m. We matched hyperspectral remote-sensing reflectance from NASA's Plankton, Aerosol, Cloud, ocean Ecosystem (PACE) mission with in situ chlorophyll estimates from Biogeochemical-Argo (BGC-Argo) floats, ocean observing moorings, and glider projects. The observations were standardized into depth bins and used to train machine-learning models that predict upper-water-column chlorophyll-a using 172 PACE reflectance bands. We evaluate the models using withheld observations and metrics describing prediction error and ecologically meaningful profile characteristics, including deep chlorophyll maximum depth, profile shape, and depth-integrated chlorophyll. Initial results show that the models reproduce profile structure and identify variation in the depth and magnitude of subsurface chlorophyll maxima. Performance is strongest in the open ocean, where most training data from BGC-Argo occur. Coastal predictions are more variable but remain informative, with performance constrained by limited coastal training data. Next steps include expanding coastal training observations, refining the representation of vertical structure, quantifying uncertainty, and deriving indicators such as chlorophyll-weighted mean depth and productive-layer thickness. We will also combine predicted profiles with mixed-layer depth fields to evaluate how phytoplankton biomass is distributed above and below the mixed layer. CHLA-Z demonstrates the potential for hyperspectral ocean color to reveal phytoplankton layers not visible in traditional surface products and support three-dimensional studies of marine ecosystems. CHLA-Z models, data products, and reproducible workflows are being developed openly at https://fish-pace.github.io/chla-z/
