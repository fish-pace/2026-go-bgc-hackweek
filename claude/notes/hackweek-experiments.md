# Hackweek experiments: what was tested and what was found

Research for issue #3 (poster story), done 2026-11-04. Sources: the final
presentation (`final_notebooks/BGC-Argo-CHLA-Z-presentation.pdf`, 28 slides),
`contributor_folders/`, the unmerged `nama` branch, `references/`, and
fish-pace/chla-z-modeling. Numbers are copied from slides or notebook outputs;
none were rerun.

## Baseline (Eli)

`notebooks/methods_fit_BRT_CHLA_z.ipynb`: 5,540 Argo + OOI profiles
matched to PACE L3 4 km Rrs (172 bands, 346–719 nm). One HistGradientBoosting
model per 10 m bin, 0–200 m. Random **row** split. Surface R² = 0.82.

## Things that conflict or need care

- **Three different evaluation setups.** Random row split (baseline, surface
  R² 0.82); float-grouped split (Nabir, 0.69); and the OLCI-overlap subset
  (0.76). Charlie's duplicate OOI rows can put the same profile in both train
  and test under a random row split, which likely inflates the 0.82. A poster
  needs one setup: deduplicated data and a float/instrument-grouped split.
- **Band count.** Slide 2 says "128 bands"; the data and abstract use 172.
- **Profile count.** "~5,500 profiles" is 5,540 rows, 4,712 unique profiles
  after removing duplicates.
- **Tier A "available globally"**, but it is unclear whether Peter computed it
  from the floats' own T/S (not deployable) or from reanalysis/climatology.
  Slide 15 has both "climatology physics" and "float physics (ceiling)".
- **Training target moved.** Retraining on today's Argo CHLA_ADJUSTED isn't
  like-for-like with the published model.
- **Spelling.** The slides and commits say Nabir Mamnun; the repo README and
  his folder README say "Nadir".

## Comparison products

- **Copernicus 3D product** = `MULTIOBS_GLO_BIO_BGC_3D_REP_015_010`, method
  SOCA2024 (QUID issue 5.0, Nov 2025, in `references/`, section II.2.1).
  - **Inputs:** Rrs at **5 bands (412, 443, 490, 555, 670 nm)**, the median
    over ±4 days and 5×5 pixels, plus the SD of Rrs412; PAR, SLA and ADT;
    the first principal component of the full T/S profile; day of year
    (sin/cos); lat/lon as x, y, z.
  - **Not surface Chl.** Surface Chl + bbp, 4 density values over the euphotic
    layer, MLD and day of year are the **SOCA2016** inputs (Sauzède et al.
    2016). SOCA2024 replaced Chl and bbp with Rrs "to avoid inherent errors
    associated with the use of ocean colour algorithms". Eli and an AI summary
    she had both described SOCA2016; the QUID is the source to cite.
  - **Model:** an ensemble of 32 MLPs for Chl. Chl is output at 50 levels down
    to 1.5 × ZNORM (ZNORM = max(MLD, 2 × Ze)), then interpolated to 36 levels.
    The ensemble spread gives `chl_error`.
  - **Training data:** 58,386 BGC-Argo stations, **2010–2024**.
  - **Product:** weekly, 0.25°, 36 levels 0–1000 m, **masked to water deeper
    than 1000 m**. The REP product runs from 1998 to "~12 to 24 months before
    present". In training the T/S comes from the float; in the gridded product
    it comes from the physical product MULTIOBS_GLO_PHY_TSUV_3D_MYNRT_015_012.
  - **Validation:** a 20% random subset of profiles (MAPD ≈ 29%), 6 independent
    floats, and HPLC (log10 Chl RMSD 0.34, r² 0.53). Dataset id
    `cmems_obs-mob_glo_bgc-chl-poc_my_0.25deg_P7D-m`.
  - **No overlap with PACE.** Checked with `copernicusmarine describe` on
    2026-11-04: the weekly dataset runs 1998-01-07 → 2023-12-27, and there is
    no near-real-time 3D chlorophyll product (the REP is the only
    `MULTIOBS_GLO_BIO_BGC_3D*`). PACE starts in March 2024, so a same-day
    comparison is impossible. See `poster/STORY.md` for the alternatives.
  - BGC-Argo profiles from 2024 may be in SOCA's training set; 2025 profiles
    are not (1,703 Argo × PACE profiles in 2025).
- **Tanner, Ewin, Viljoen & Brewin (2024)**, *Front. Remote Sens.* 5,
  doi:10.3389/frsen.2024.1495958. Surface Chl → column-integrated Chl in the
  euphotic zone; no ML (nonlinear least squares); MODIS 9 km seasonal
  climatology (Chl, Kd) plus MIMOC MLD; 76,043 BGC-Argo profiles (922 floats,
  2010–2023); time split at 2022-04-21. It improves on Uitz et al. (2006).
  Table 2 values came from a summary and should be checked on the paper
  before quoting.
